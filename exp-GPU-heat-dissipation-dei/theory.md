# Theory

## 1. Introduction

Modern computing systems generate significant amounts of heat during operation. As computing performance increases, managing this heat becomes critical for maintaining reliability, efficiency, and hardware lifespan. Graphics Processing Units (GPUs), which are widely used in Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning (ML), scientific simulations, and high-performance computing, are among the highest power-consuming components in modern computing systems.

This experiment investigates how heat is generated inside a GPU, how it is removed through the cooling system, and how various factors such as ambient temperature, power consumption, thermal resistance, and cooling failures affect GPU performance.

---

# 2. Graphics Processing Units (GPUs)

A Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) is a specialized processor designed to perform many calculations simultaneously.

Unlike a Central Processing Unit (CPU), which contains a small number of powerful cores optimized for sequential tasks, a GPU contains thousands of smaller processing cores optimized for parallel computation.

Because thousands of cores operate simultaneously, GPUs are capable of processing large amounts of data efficiently. This makes them ideal for:

- Artificial Intelligence (AI)
- Machine Learning (ML)
- Deep Learning
- Scientific Simulations
- Data Analytics
- Computer Graphics
- High-Performance Computing (HPC)

However, this computational capability comes at the cost of high power consumption and significant heat generation.

---

# 3. GPUs in Modern Data Centers

Modern cloud computing platforms rely heavily on GPU-accelerated servers.

A data center is a specialized facility designed to house thousands of servers and computing devices while providing:

- Electrical power
- Network connectivity
- Environmental control
- Physical security

A modern data center consists of three major subsystems:

## 3.1 Compute Infrastructure

This includes:

- CPUs
- GPUs
- Storage devices
- Networking hardware

These components perform the computational work.

## 3.2 Power Infrastructure

This subsystem ensures uninterrupted operation through:

- Power distribution units (PDUs)
- Uninterruptible power supplies (UPS)
- Backup generators

## 3.3 Cooling Infrastructure

The cooling infrastructure removes heat generated by the hardware.

Examples include:

- HVAC systems
- Air-cooling systems
- Liquid-cooling systems
- Chilled water systems

Without adequate cooling, data center hardware would quickly exceed safe operating temperatures.

---

# 4. GPU Cooling System Components

The cooling system of a GPU consists of several components that work together to remove heat from the processor.

## 4.1 GPU Die

The GPU die is the central silicon chip that performs all computational operations.

Characteristics:

- Contains thousands of processing cores
- Consumes electrical power
- Generates heat during operation
- Acts as the primary heat source in the system

In this experiment, the GPU die is the origin of all thermal energy.

---

## 4.2 Thermal Paste

Thermal paste, also known as Thermal Interface Material (TIM), is applied between the GPU die and the heatsink.

Purpose:

- Fills microscopic air gaps
- Improves thermal contact
- Increases heat-transfer efficiency

Since air is a poor conductor of heat, eliminating air gaps significantly improves cooling performance.

---

## 4.3 Heatsink

A heatsink is a metal structure designed to absorb and dissipate heat.

It is typically made from:

- Aluminum
- Copper
- Copper heat pipes with aluminum fins

Functions:

- Absorbs heat from the GPU die
- Distributes heat over a large area
- Transfers heat to the surrounding air

Increasing the surface area improves heat dissipation.

---

## 4.4 Cooling Fans

Cooling fans force air through the heatsink fins.

Functions:

- Increase airflow
- Improve convective heat transfer
- Remove heated air from the system

Higher fan speeds generally improve cooling performance by increasing the rate of heat removal.

---

## 4.5 PCB (Printed Circuit Board)

The PCB forms the foundation of the GPU.

It contains:

- Electrical traces
- Power delivery circuitry
- Memory modules
- Communication interfaces

Although the PCB carries electrical signals and power, it is not the primary heat-dissipation component.

---

# 5. Heat Transfer Path

Heat generated inside the GPU follows a specific path before reaching the environment.

The primary heat-transfer path is:

```text
GPU Die
    ↓
Thermal Paste
    ↓
Heatsink
    ↓
Cooling Air
    ↓
Environment
```

The objective of the cooling system is to minimize resistance along this path so that heat can be removed efficiently.

---

# 6. Thermal Resistance

Thermal resistance is a measure of how difficult it is for heat to flow through a material or interface.

It is represented by:

\[
R_{\theta}
\]

and is measured in:

\[
°C/W
\]

( Degrees Celsius per Watt )

### Low Thermal Resistance

- Efficient heat transfer
- Better cooling performance
- Lower temperatures

### High Thermal Resistance

- Reduced heat transfer
- Poor cooling performance
- Higher temperatures

Thermal resistance is one of the most important parameters in thermal system design.

---

# 7. Ambient Temperature

Ambient temperature refers to the temperature of the surrounding environment.

In a data center, this corresponds to the temperature of the air around the servers.

Higher ambient temperatures reduce cooling effectiveness because the temperature difference between the GPU and its surroundings becomes smaller.

As ambient temperature increases:

- Cooling becomes less effective
- Junction temperature rises
- Thermal throttling becomes more likely

---

# 8. GPU Power Consumption

The power consumed by a GPU is converted into useful computation and heat.

As workload increases:

- Power consumption increases
- Heat generation increases
- Junction temperature rises

Modern enterprise GPUs may consume between:

- 200 W and 700 W

depending on workload and architecture.

---

# 9. Junction Temperature

The Junction Temperature (\(T_j\)) is the temperature of the GPU silicon die.

It is one of the most important operating parameters because it directly affects:

- Performance
- Reliability
- Lifespan

The cooling system is designed to keep the junction temperature below safe operating limits.

---

# 10. Thermal Balance Equation

The relationship between power consumption and temperature can be approximated using the thermal balance equation:

T<sub>j</sub> = T<sub>a</sub> + (P &times; R<sub>&theta;</sub>)

Where:

| Symbol | Description |
|----------|------------|
| T<sub>j</sub> | Junction Temperature (°C) |
| T<sub>a</sub> | Ambient Temperature (°C) |
| P | Power Draw (W) |
| R<sub>&theta;</sub>| Thermal Resistance (°C/W) |

This equation forms the foundation of the simulation.

---

# 11. Thermal Throttling

GPUs contain built-in protection mechanisms to prevent overheating.

When the junction temperature exceeds a safe limit (typically around 85°C), the GPU activates thermal throttling.

Thermal throttling works by:

- Reducing clock speeds
- Lowering voltage
- Reducing power consumption

As a result:

- Heat generation decreases
- Temperature stabilizes
- Compute performance drops

Thermal throttling protects the hardware from damage but reduces computational throughput.

---

# 12. Thermal Paste Degradation

Over time, thermal paste can degrade due to:

- Drying
- Pump-out effects
- Mechanical stress
- Thermal cycling

This degradation creates microscopic air gaps between the die and heatsink.

Consequences:

- Increased thermal resistance
- Poor heat transfer
- Higher junction temperatures
- Earlier thermal throttling

The simulation includes a thermal paste degradation scenario to demonstrate this effect.

---

# 13. HVAC Failure Scenario

Data centers rely heavily on HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning) systems.

An HVAC failure can cause:

- Increased ambient temperature
- Reduced cooling effectiveness
- Elevated junction temperatures

Even if GPU workload remains unchanged, an increase in ambient temperature directly increases junction temperature according to the thermal balance equation.

This scenario demonstrates the importance of environmental control in large-scale computing facilities.
